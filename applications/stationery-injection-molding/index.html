Stationery Injection Molding

Stationery — pen bodies, caps, clips, sharpeners, correction-tape shells — is small precision molding at very high volume. Economics live on cycle time, cavitation and color consistency.

High cavitation, short cycles

Pen caps and clips run in 16-64 cavity molds on high-speed machines. At that cavitation, one second of cycle time is worth thousands of pieces per shift — the same logic as thin-wall packaging.

Materials

PS, PP and ABS dominate stationery. Small shot sizes demand precise injection control — a machine that is oversized for the shot runs unstable at tiny doses, which shows up as weight variation across the cavity set.

Assembly considerations

Stationery parts assemble without fasteners: snap fits, threads and press fits molded in. Tolerance across the whole cavity set matters — cavity-to-cavity variation shows up as caps that fit on some cavities and not others, which is a mold-steel and process-stability question.

Frequently asked questions

What cavity counts are typical for stationery?

Pen caps and clips commonly run 16-64 cavity molds on high-speed machines; at that cavitation, seconds of cycle time translate to thousands of pieces per shift.

What causes shade drift in colored stationery?

Masterbatch dosing inconsistency and barrel-temperature variation. Consistent dosing and stable temperature control keep shades aligned across shifts.

Why does my machine run unstable on small shots?

An oversized machine at a tiny shot dose runs near the bottom of its control range. Matching the machine size to the shot weight stabilizes small-part molding.
