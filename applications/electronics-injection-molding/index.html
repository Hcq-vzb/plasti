Electronics Injection Molding

Electronics housings and connectors are thin-walled, cosmetically critical and dimensionally tight. The reject rate lives or dies on shot-to-shot repeatability.

Thin-wall precision

Connector housings and mobile-device parts push wall thickness down to fractions of a millimetre, which demands high injection speed and precise velocity control — the transition from fill to pack must happen at exactly the right point or the part short-shots or flashes.

Materials

PC, ABS and PC-ABS dominate; PBT and PA for connectors requiring heat resistance. All are hygroscopic — drying discipline is the cheapest reject-rate reduction in electronics molding.

Frequently asked questions

Why do electronics parts need such precise fill-to-pack transition?

Thin walls fill in milliseconds. If the machine switches from fill to pack pressure late, the part flashes; early, it short-shoots. Velocity control precision sets the reject rate.

What causes flow marks on glossy housings?

Usually melt-temperature instability, gate design or injection speed profile — machine-side stability plus mold polish together control the cosmetic result.

Are hygroscopic materials really that sensitive?

Yes. PC, ABS and PA absorb moisture that turns to steam in the barrel, causing silver streaks and brittle parts. Drying to specification is the cheapest quality improvement.
