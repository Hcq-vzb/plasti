Shoe Material Injection Molding

Footwear soles are a specialized corner of injection molding: PVC, PU and TPR soles are molded on dedicated machines with rotary tables and multi-station tooling, not on standard horizontal presses.

Why footwear needs specialized machines

A shoe sole mold is a pair of matching dies clamped around a last, cycling in a carousel. Rotary multi-station machines let operators load and unload while other stations mold — the throughput of shoe production depends on this overlap, which standard horizontal machines do not provide.

What to specify

  • Station count and rotary configuration matched to your daily output
  • Material dosing accuracy — sole weight variation is a visible quality issue
  • Mold changeover time between sizes and models
  • Heating-zone control for consistent expansion and surface finish
PU soles are a different process family (reactive mixing). Confirm the material route before comparing machines — a PVC/TPR injection machine and a PU pouring line are not interchangeable.

Frequently asked questions

Why are shoe sole machines rotary?

Sole molds are pairs of dies around a last, cycled on a carousel so operators load and unload while other stations mold — throughput depends on that overlap.

Which materials are used for soles?

PVC and TPR for injected soles, PU for midsoles and safety footwear, and foamed EVA/CR for comfort midsoles — each with a dedicated machine process.

Is PU sole production the same as PVC injection?

No. PU is a reactive mixing process with its own dosing equipment; a PVC/TPR injection machine cannot be converted to PU production.
