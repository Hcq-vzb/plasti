Auto Parts Injection Molding

Automotive parts are unforgiving: tolerances are tight, materials are glass-filled or elastomer-modified, and a rejected batch can stop a customer's assembly line. Machine selection follows from those three facts.

What automotive parts demand from the machine

  • Repeatability over peak speed. A stable process window beats a fast cycle: part weight variation is what triggers customer complaints.
  • Packing control. Structural parts (brackets, housings) need precise holding-pressure profiling to control sink marks and warpage.
  • Wear resistance. Glass-filled PP and PA6.6 are abrasive; hardened screw and barrel options should be specified.

Frequently asked questions

Which machine type suits automotive interior parts?

Precision-intelligent or all-electric machines give the closed-loop shot and pack control that cosmetic interior parts need; machine selection should follow the customer's part-weight tolerance, not cycle time alone.

Do glass-filled materials damage the machine?

Glass fibers are abrasive. Hardened screw and barrel options and regular wear checks keep shot-weight stability over long runs.

Can one machine run both rigid and TPE overmolding?

Two-material production needs a dedicated two-shot configuration or a rotary-mold process; a standard machine runs the rigid substrate and overmolding is a separate step.
