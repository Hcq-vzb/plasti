Screw And Barrel for Injection Molding

Published 2026-11-25 · KIWL Machinery

Three questions decide most screw and barrel for injection molding purchases, and they are the same for every injection molding shops operation: what rate, what accuracy, and what happens when it stops. This guide takes them in that order.

What This Equipment Does

Because a screw and barrel for injection molding handles a defined step for injection molding shops, it can be isolated, measured and improved. This build is specified for 16 oz product at 6554–9554 units per hour, with changeover handled by configuration rather than by hand.

Key Technical Specifications

The specification that gets missed is the changeover. A screw and barrel for injection molding with a 12-minute changeover across 16 oz format and food-grade 304 stainless steel contact parts is a different proposition from one without, even at the same 6554–9554 units per hour.

Integration With Your Line

Buffer sizing upstream of the screw and barrel for injection molding determines whether the line absorbs a changeover. With 6554–9554 units per hour and a 12-minute format change, the buffer has to cover the stop, not the run.

Capacity Planning and ROI

Run the ROI on the worst month, not the average one. A screw and barrel for injection molding rated at 6554–9554 units per hour will look different in peak season than in a quiet month, and the investment case has to survive both.

Maintenance and Service

Wear items on a screw and barrel for injection molding are known and finite. Planning the injection molding shops operation around lead times for nozzle, check ring and heater means the line is not waiting a week for a part while the shift runs.

Troubleshooting: Common Faults and How to Resolve Them

SymptomLikely causeCorrective action
Cycle time drifting longerHydraulic oil heating up over a long runCheck the oil cooler and the hydraulic filter, and log the cycle time per shot
Weld lines visible on the partFlow front splitting around the coreMove the gate to the weld line or raise the injection speed within the machine limit
Short shot on thin wallsMelt temperature too high or hold pressure lowReduce the melt temperature by 5–8 °C and check the hold pressure profile against the gate
Nozzle drool during idleResidual pressure in the screwRun the decompression cycle and verify the back-pressure setting

Frequently Asked Questions

What maintenance does it need?

Routine checks on the wear items and a scheduled service at the recommended interval.

Can the machine run different moulds?

Yes, the machine is set up for tool changeover; the parameter backup is stored per tool.

What clamping force do I need?

It follows the projected area of the part and the resin's flow rate; the reference build covers 6554–9554 kN.

How do I size this for my plant?

Base capacity on peak hourly demand plus 15–20% headroom, and confirm utility availability before ordering.

Does it integrate with existing lines?

Yes, with standard PLC interfaces and shared CIP or control logic when specified at quotation.

What is typical lead time and service?

Standard machines ship in weeks; remote commissioning and spare parts support are usually included.
